BB84, photon by photon

BB84 is a way for Alice and Bob to agree a secret key by sending single particles of light, photons. Each photon carries one bit, 0 or 1, set one of two ways called bases: + (upright or flat) or x (diagonal). Bob measures each photon in a basis he picks at random and they keep only the bits where his basis matched hers. Press Play to send the 24 example photons one after another.

      The numbers as a table

      Each point is one whole exchange with the chosen number of photons, with the error limit switched off so the rate falls to zero by itself. The ideal curve is 0.375 (1 − 2h(e)), where e is the share of bits wrong and h is the binary entropy, the information in a coin that lands one way with chance e: comparing bases and checking a sample keep 0.375 of the photons, and 1 − 2h(e) is what is left after perfect error correction and hiding what Eve could know.

      Computed by the same Rust code as the command line tool, compiled to WebAssembly. The photons are simulated: this shows the protocol, not the physics of a real link.
